# SCOUT Report: Hellstar Visual Identity (for the Statement Graphics niche pod)

**Source caveat:** Hellstar has little formal brand press — findings come from resale/authentication guides and streetwear blogs, not brand editorial. Treat specifics as reasonably consistent but not brand-official.

## Visual identity
LA streetwear brand, "dark spiritual/cosmic streetwear" — gothic lettering, star/celestial imagery, flames, skulls, apocalyptic symbolism. Heavyweight oversized garments, boxy dropped-shoulder cut, garment-dyed/washed finishes.

## Placement conventions
Large-scale full-panel back prints as the signature move — the oversized cut is deliberately used as a canvas. Layered, dense compositions (multiple motifs combined), often extending onto sleeves. Chest hits are secondary to the dominant back piece.

## What separates "high-end" from cheap clipart
Plastisol screen printing (dense, opaque ink) vs. thin DTG. Sharp edges and fine detail retention even in complex art. Heavyweight garment-dyed blanks, not thin/papery tees. Tight even stitching. Intentional (not sloppy) distressing — texture designed into the artwork, not degraded print quality.

## Contrast with Fear of God Essentials
Essentials = restraint-as-luxury (small tonal logo, no imagery). Hellstar = the opposite lever — value communicated through graphic maximalism and print craft. PRISM's brief: borrow Hellstar's boldness (real imagery, scale, presence), import Essentials/Kith's restraint in *quantity and taste* (one strong graphic moment, not clutter).

## SCOUT's judgment call
Leave heavy distressing/vintage-wash **out** of this premium line — it reads as "worn streetwear," not premium, and can undercut the high-end goal. What should carry over is Hellstar's conviction: scale, confidence, genuinely illustrated artwork — not the grunge/distressing signature.

## Art-direction brief (executed by PRISM)
One dominant, large-scale illustrated graphic (painterly/linework, not stock icon or font lockup), restrained to 1-2 ink colors on a heavyweight garment-dyed blank, printed with maximum density/opacity so detail survives at scale, zero distressing on the base garment, nothing else on the garment — one confident illustrated statement per piece.

## Status
Research complete. PRISM's first Statement Graphics design (an illustrated compass + phoenix emblem in gold ink on black) is in production now.
