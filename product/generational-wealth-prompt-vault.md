# The Generational Wealth Prompt Vault

**A Wealth Architect HQ product.** The AI companion to The Wealth Architect System, built for anyone ready to put AI to work on their money instead of just chatting with it.

---

## Welcome

Everybody's using AI to write emails and captions. Almost nobody is using it to actually build wealth. That's the gap this vault closes.

Inside are 70 prompts, organized into 7 categories that cover the real order of operations for building financial freedom: get your cash flow right, kill your debt, grow your income, build something that pays you back, understand investing without getting scammed, get paid what you're worth, and protect what you build.

Every prompt follows the same structure: a role for the AI to act as, a clear task, and bracketed fill ins for your specific numbers and situation. Copy, fill in the brackets, paste into ChatGPT or Claude, and you get a real answer built around your life, not a generic listicle.

## How to use this

1. Pick the category that matches what you're working on right now. Don't try to run all 70 at once.
2. Fill in every bracket with your real numbers and real situation. The more specific you are, the better the output.
3. Treat the AI's answer as a first draft of a plan, not a final verdict. Reread it, push back on anything that doesn't fit your life, and ask a follow up question if something's unclear.
4. For anything touching investing, insurance, estate planning, or taxes: use these prompts to get educated and get organized, then bring the output to a licensed professional before you act on it. That's not a legal disclaimer stuck at the bottom, it's the actual right way to use AI for money decisions that carry real risk.

## The one rule

**This is not financial, legal, or tax advice, and it's not a replacement for a licensed advisor.** These prompts are tools for thinking clearly and getting organized. Anything with real financial stakes (investing specific amounts, filing taxes, drafting legal documents) should go through a licensed professional who knows your full situation. Use the Vault to show up to that conversation prepared, not to skip it.

## Category 1: Budgeting & Cash Flow

Builds on the Wealth Split framework from The Wealth Architect System (the 50/20/20/10 paycheck breakdown). Start here if you don't know where your money actually goes every month.

**1.1: Build your split**
> Act as a personal finance coach. My monthly take home pay is [$amount]. My fixed bills are [list them with amounts]. Using a needs, savings, debt, and freedom percentage breakdown, show me exactly how much should go where, and flag anything in my fixed bills that looks too high for my income.

**1.2: Find the leak**
> Act as a budget auditor. Here's everywhere my money went last month: [list categories and amounts, or paste a bank statement summary]. Identify the top 3 categories where I'm overspending relative to my income of [$amount], and suggest one specific change for each that I could actually stick to.

**1.3: Build a bare bones budget**
> Act as a financial planner. I need to survive on the absolute minimum for [number] months because [reason, e.g. job loss, starting a business]. My essential fixed costs are [list them]. Build me a bare bones monthly budget that keeps me current on essentials and tells me exactly what to cut.

**1.4: Irregular income budget**
> Act as a cash flow specialist for freelancers and business owners. My income varies month to month, usually between [$low amount] and [$high amount]. Build me a system for budgeting off my lowest realistic month and handling the extra when a bigger month comes in.

**1.5: Subscription audit**
> Act as a cost cutting expert. Here's every subscription and recurring charge I have: [list them with amounts]. Rank them by value versus cost, tell me which ones to cut first, and estimate my total annual savings if I cut the bottom half.

**1.6: Emergency fund target**
> Act as a financial planner. My essential monthly expenses are [$amount] and my job stability is [describe: stable salary, commission, freelance, etc.]. Tell me a realistic emergency fund target for my situation and a month by month plan to reach it from my current savings of [$amount].

**1.7: Sinking funds for irregular expenses**
> Act as a budgeting coach. I keep getting blindsided by expenses that aren't monthly, like [list examples: car registration, holidays, annual insurance]. Build me a sinking fund system that breaks each one into a monthly savings amount so I'm never caught off guard again.

**1.8: Couples money conversation**
> Act as a financial counselor who specializes in couples. My partner and I have different spending habits: [describe the difference]. Give me a script for a calm, non blaming conversation about merging our budgeting approach, plus 3 questions we should answer together before we talk about numbers.

**1.9: 30 day spending reset**
> Act as an accountability coach. I want to reset my spending habits for 30 days. My biggest weakness is [describe, e.g. impulse buying, eating out]. Build me a day by day 30 day challenge that retrains this specific habit without feeling like punishment.

**1.10: Annual money review**
> Act as a financial reviewer. At the end of each year I want to look back honestly. Give me a list of the 10 most important questions to ask myself about my money from the past 12 months, covering spending, saving, debt, and income, so I can walk into next year with a real plan instead of a vague resolution.

## Category 2: Debt Payoff

Builds on the Debt Freedom Ladder framework from The Wealth Architect System (the 4 step payoff order). Match and interest language stays general here on purpose. Your actual lender terms are the only accurate source for your real numbers.

**2.1: Build my payoff order**
> Act as a debt payoff strategist. Here are all my debts: [list each with balance, interest rate, and minimum payment]. Using both the avalanche method (highest interest first) and the snowball method (smallest balance first), show me both payoff orders and tell me which one fits my situation better given that I [describe: need quick wins for motivation / want to save the most on interest].

**2.2: Debt free timeline**
> Act as a debt payoff calculator and coach. My total debt is [$amount] across [number] accounts. I can put [$amount] extra toward debt each month beyond minimums. Build me a realistic month by month timeline to debt freedom and tell me what would happen to that timeline if I added another [$amount] per month.

**2.3: Negotiate a lower rate**
> Act as a consumer advocate who has negotiated with creditors before. I want to call my [credit card / loan] company and ask for a lower interest rate. My account has been open [length of time] and my payment history is [describe]. Write me a calm, confident script for that call, including what to say if they say no the first time.

**2.4: Consolidation decision**
> Act as an unbiased debt advisor. I'm considering consolidating my debts (currently [list balances and rates]) into a [balance transfer card / personal loan / other option]. List the real pros and cons of doing this for my specific numbers, and tell me exactly what math I need to do before deciding, without telling me what to pick.

**2.5: Talking to collections**
> Act as a consumer rights coach. A collections agency is contacting me about a debt of [$amount]. Explain what my basic rights are in plain language, and give me a script for responding that protects me without me having to sound confrontational.

**2.6: Student loan payoff strategy**
> Act as a student loan specialist. I have [$amount] in student loans at [interest rate], and my income is [$amount]. Explain the realistic options available for someone in my situation (standard repayment, income based options, refinancing) in plain terms, and tell me what questions I need to answer before choosing one.

**2.7: Debt payoff motivation system**
> Act as a behavior change coach. I've tried to pay off debt before and lost motivation around [describe when it usually falls apart, e.g. month 3]. Design a tracking and reward system for my current payoff plan that keeps me motivated specifically past that point.

**2.8: Should I pause investing to pay off debt**
> Act as a financial educator. I have [$amount] in debt at [interest rate] and I'm also contributing [$amount or %] to retirement. Walk me through the general framework people use to decide between prioritizing debt payoff versus continuing to invest (matching, interest rate comparisons, risk tolerance) so I can bring an informed question to a financial advisor.

**2.9: Explaining debt to family**
> Act as a communication coach. I need to tell [family member] that I'm dealing with [$amount] in debt, and I've been avoiding the conversation because [reason]. Help me write out what I want to say so it's honest without spiraling into shame or over explaining.

**2.10: Post payoff plan**
> Act as a financial planner. I'm on track to be debt free in [timeframe]. Once that monthly payment amount of [$amount] is freed up, help me build a plan for where it should go next (emergency fund, investing, specific goal) based on my current savings of [$amount] and goals of [describe].

**3.1: Find your market** *(from the original AI Prompt Playbook)*
> Act as a market researcher. I'm starting a [type of business] targeting [audience]. Identify my top 3 competitors, what they do well, where they fall short, and one gap I could fill that they're not addressing.

**3.2: Content plan** *(from the original AI Prompt Playbook)*
> Act as a content strategist. My business is [describe]. My audience is [describe]. Give me 4 content pillars and 5 post ideas for each that would actually get engagement, not generic advice.

**3.3: 30 day launch plan** *(from the original AI Prompt Playbook)*
> Act as a launch strategist. I'm about to launch [product]. Give me a day by day 30 day plan from pre launch to first sale, including what to post, when, and the goal of each week.

**3.4: Side hustle idea audit**
> Act as a business advisor. Here are the skills I already have: [list them]. Here's the time I have available: [hours per week]. Give me 5 realistic side hustle ideas that fit both, ranked by how fast each one could realistically produce its first dollar.

**3.5: Price your time**
> Act as a pricing consultant for freelancers. I offer [service]. My experience level is [describe]. My target income from this is [$amount per month]. Tell me a realistic hourly or project rate range and show me the math for how many clients or hours that actually requires.

**3.6: First 5 clients plan**
> Act as a sales coach for new freelancers. I just started offering [service] and have zero clients. Give me a specific, unpolished but realistic plan for getting my first 5 paying clients using only the network and platforms I already have access to: [list what you have, e.g. Instagram, LinkedIn, local community].

**3.7: Turn a skill into a digital product**
> Act as a digital product strategist. I'm good at [skill or knowledge area]. Walk me through what a simple first digital product (guide, template, mini course) built around this skill could look like, who it's for, and what I'd need to create before launch.

**3.8: Negotiate a raise**
> Act as a career coach. I want to ask for a raise. My current role is [title], my key accomplishments this year are [list them], and market rate for my role appears to be around [$amount if known, or "unknown"]. Build me a talking point outline for that conversation, including how to respond if they say the budget isn't there.

**3.9: Diversify income streams**
> Act as an income strategist. My only current income source is [describe, e.g. my 9 to 5 salary]. Based on my skills ([list them]) and available time ([hours per week]), suggest a realistic second income stream I could build over the next 6 months without burning out.

**3.10: Weekly income review**
> Act as an accountability partner. I'm building a new income stream: [describe]. Give me 5 specific questions I should ask myself every week to track real progress (not vanity metrics) and catch early if something isn't working.

## Category 4: Business & Offer Building

Reuses the proven offer and positioning prompts, plus new ones for building the full package around what you sell.

**4.1: Build the offer** *(from the original AI Prompt Playbook)*
> Act as a pricing and offer strategist. Here's what I'm selling: [describe it]. Build me a value stack, the core offer plus bonuses, and suggest 3 price points with the reasoning behind each.

**4.2: Position against competitors** *(from the original AI Prompt Playbook)*
> Act as a brand strategist. Compare my business [describe] against these competitors [list them]. Tell me exactly how to position myself so I don't sound like a copy of them.

**4.3: Name and identity check**
> Act as a branding consultant. I'm considering the name [proposed name] for my business, which does [describe]. Tell me how it reads to a first time customer, whether it's easily confused with something else, and whether it will still make sense if the business grows beyond its current offer.

**4.4: Write the sales page outline**
> Act as a direct response copywriter. My offer is [describe it, price, and who it's for]. Build me a sales page outline using a proven structure (hook, problem, solution, proof, offer stack, guarantee if applicable, call to action) with a one line description of what goes in each section for my specific offer.

**4.5: Handle the top objection**
> Act as a sales trainer. The number one objection I hear about my offer [describe it] is [describe the objection, e.g. "it's too expensive," "I can just find this free online"]. Give me 3 different honest ways to respond to that objection without being pushy or dishonest.

**4.6: Build a simple guarantee**
> Act as a business advisor. My offer is [describe it and its price]. Help me think through whether a guarantee makes sense for this specific offer, and if so, design one that protects me from abuse while still removing real buying risk for the customer.

**4.7: Turn one offer into a value ladder**
> Act as a funnel strategist. My current offer is [describe it and its price]. Sketch out what a simple value ladder could look like above and below this offer (a cheaper entry point and a bigger upgrade), based on what would be a natural next step for someone who already bought this.

**4.8: Write your elevator pitch**
> Act as a pitch coach. My business is [describe]. My ideal customer is [describe]. Write me 3 versions of a 10 second answer to "what do you do" for 3 different situations: a networking event, a social media bio, and a cold introduction to a potential customer.

**4.9: Vet a business partnership**
> Act as a business advisor. I'm considering partnering with [describe the person or company] on [describe the deal]. List the key questions I should get answered before agreeing to anything, and flag anything about this specific arrangement that sounds like it needs to go through a lawyer before I sign.

**4.10: Simplify your offer**
> Act as a business consultant. Here's everything I currently offer: [list all products or services]. Tell me honestly if this is too many things for one brand to be known for, and if so, suggest which one I should lead with while the others become secondary.

## Category 5: Investing Basics (Education Only)

These prompts explain concepts so you understand what you're looking at. They never pick a stock, a fund, or a specific dollar amount to invest. Anything with real money on the line goes through a licensed financial advisor who knows your full situation.

**5.1: Explain index funds like I'm new**
> Act as a financial educator. Explain what an index fund is, how it's different from picking individual stocks, and why they're commonly recommended for long term investors, using plain language and no jargon.

**5.2: Explain compound interest with my own numbers**
> Act as a financial educator. Using an example of [$amount] invested at a [%] average annual return over [number] years, show me the math of compound growth step by step so I understand why starting early matters, using this purely as an illustrative example, not a prediction.

**5.3: Understand my risk tolerance**
> Act as a financial educator. Walk me through the general factors that go into figuring out someone's investment risk tolerance (time horizon, income stability, how they'd react to a market drop) and give me questions to think through about my own situation, without telling me what to actually invest in.

**5.4: Retirement account basics**
> Act as a financial educator. Explain the general differences between the common types of retirement accounts (like a 401k and an IRA) in plain terms: how they're typically funded, how they're typically taxed, and what general questions I should ask my employer or a financial advisor about my own specific plan.

**5.5: What is diversification, really**
> Act as a financial educator. Explain what diversification actually means in investing, why it matters, and a simple analogy that makes it click for someone who's never invested before.

**5.6: Understand fees I'm paying**
> Act as a financial educator. Explain the common types of fees that come with investment accounts (expense ratios, advisor fees, account fees) and why even small looking percentages matter over a long time horizon, using general math to illustrate, not specific product recommendations.

**5.7: Decode financial jargon**
> Act as a financial educator. Here's a list of investing terms I keep seeing and don't fully understand: [list them, e.g. "expense ratio," "bull market," "dollar cost averaging"]. Explain each one in one plain sentence a beginner could actually use in conversation.

**5.8: Prepare questions for a financial advisor**
> Act as a financial educator preparing me for a meeting with a licensed financial advisor. My general situation is [describe: age range, income range, goals]. Give me a list of smart questions to bring to that meeting so I get the most out of the professional's time.

**5.9: Understand market volatility**
> Act as a financial educator. Explain in plain language why markets go up and down in the short term, why that's historically normal for long term investors, and what emotional traps (like panic selling) tend to hurt people the most during volatile periods.

**5.10: Build my own investing education plan**
> Act as a financial educator. I know almost nothing about investing and want to learn before I talk to a professional. Build me a simple learning path (in order) of core concepts I should understand first, so I can walk into that conversation informed instead of guessing.

## Category 6: Career & Income Negotiation

**6.1: Prep for a salary negotiation**
> Act as a career coach. I have a job offer of [$amount] for [role]. My research shows market rate is around [$amount, or "unclear"]. Help me build a negotiation script, including a specific counter number and how to justify it.

**6.2: Resume rewrite for impact**
> Act as a resume writer. Here's my current bullet point: [paste it]. Rewrite it to lead with a measurable result instead of a duty description, and explain the difference so I can apply the same fix to the rest of my resume.

**6.3: Interview story bank**
> Act as an interview coach. I have an interview for [role] coming up. Here's my work history: [summarize it]. Help me identify 3 specific stories from my background I should have ready, each mapped to a common interview question (a challenge I overcame, a time I led something, a mistake I learned from).

**6.4: Answer "what's your salary expectation"**
> Act as a negotiation coach. I'm being asked my salary expectation for a role paying an estimated [$amount range]. Give me 3 different ways to answer that question that protect my negotiating position without being evasive or dishonest.

**6.5: Career pivot plan**
> Act as a career strategist. I currently work in [current field] and want to move into [target field]. Identify the transferable skills I already have, the gap skills I'm missing, and a realistic step by step plan to make the move over [timeframe].

**6.6: LinkedIn profile audit**
> Act as a personal branding coach. Here's my current LinkedIn headline and summary: [paste them]. Rewrite both to clearly communicate what I do and who I help, in a way that would make a recruiter or client want to click into my profile.

**6.7: Ask for a promotion**
> Act as a career coach. I believe I'm ready for a promotion to [target role]. My key accomplishments in my current role are [list them]. Build me a conversation outline for raising this with my manager, including how to handle "not yet, but soon."

**6.8: Freelance rate increase**
> Act as a business coach for freelancers. I currently charge [$amount] for [service] and want to raise my rate to [$amount] for new clients. Give me a script for introducing the new rate to existing clients without losing them, and a separate script for quoting new clients at the higher rate with full confidence.

**6.9: Handle a counteroffer**
> Act as a career advisor. I resigned from my current job and they came back with a counteroffer of [$amount or describe the offer]. Walk me through the honest factors people usually regret ignoring when deciding whether to accept a counteroffer versus leaving anyway.

**6.10: Build a personal income roadmap**
> Act as a career strategist. My current income is [$amount] and my goal is [$amount] within [timeframe]. Map out the realistic combination of paths (raise, promotion, job change, side income) that could actually close that gap, and which one to focus on first.

## Category 7: Protecting What You Build

Insurance and estate planning are two of the easiest things to get catastrophically wrong by guessing. These prompts are built to get you organized and informed, then hand you off to a licensed professional, exactly the way this whole category is meant to work.

**7.1: Insurance gap check**
> Act as a financial educator. Here's my current situation: [describe: age, dependents, homeowner or renter, current insurance coverage if known]. Explain in general terms the common types of insurance people in my situation typically need to think about (health, life, disability, property), and what questions I should ask a licensed insurance agent about each.

**7.2: Do I need life insurance**
> Act as a financial educator. Explain in plain language the general factors people consider when deciding if they need life insurance (dependents, debts, income replacement) so I can think through my own situation, without telling me a specific coverage amount or product to buy.

**7.3: Understand a will versus a trust**
> Act as a financial educator. Explain in plain language the general difference between a will and a trust, why people commonly use one, the other, or both, and what questions I should bring to an estate planning attorney about my own situation.

**7.4: Organize my important documents**
> Act as an organization consultant. Build me a checklist of the important financial and legal documents a person should have gathered and accessible in one place (examples: insurance policies, account information, identification, any existing legal documents), so my family wouldn't be scrambling to find things in an emergency.

**7.5: Talk to my family about a plan**
> Act as a family communication coach. I want to talk to [family member, e.g. my parents, my spouse] about getting our affairs in order (wills, beneficiaries, important documents) but the topic feels heavy. Give me a gentle way to open that conversation that doesn't sound like I'm assuming the worst.

**7.6: Beneficiary check**
> Act as a financial organizer. Explain why beneficiary designations on accounts like retirement accounts and life insurance policies matter, how they can override what's written in a will, and remind me of the general life events that should trigger a beneficiary review.

**7.7: Protect a side business**
> Act as a business advisor. I'm running a side business as [describe: sole proprietor, LLC, etc.]. Explain in general terms why business structure and liability protection matter, and what questions I should bring to a lawyer or accountant about protecting my personal assets from business risk.

**7.8: Identity and fraud protection basics**
> Act as a security educator. Explain the basic steps a person should take to protect their financial identity (things like credit monitoring, freezing credit, recognizing common scams), in plain, non alarmist language.

**7.9: Prepare for a professional consultation**
> Act as an organization coach. I'm about to meet with a [financial advisor / estate attorney / insurance agent] for the first time. Help me build a one page summary of my situation and questions to bring, so the meeting is efficient and I don't forget anything important.

**7.10: Build a "protect what I built" annual checklist**
> Act as a financial organizer. Build me a once a year checklist for reviewing the protective side of my finances (insurance coverage, beneficiaries, important documents, any legal updates needed), so this doesn't get forgotten between the big life events that usually trigger it.
